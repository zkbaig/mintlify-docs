## Learning Outcomes
By the end of this course, students will be able to: Assess venture opportunities: Analyze the viability of startup business opportunities by evaluating market needs, product/technology fit, founding team capabilities, competitive landscape, and growth potential using criteria that venture investors apply in screening and due diligence. Develop projections and valuations: Construct realistic financial projections for early-stage ventures (revenue, expenses, cash burn, etc.) and apply startup valuation methods (venture capital method, discounted cash flow, comparables) to estimate company value and potential investor returns under various scenarios. Strategize financing options: Identify and compare appropriate financing sources (e.g. personal funding, angel investors, venture capital, crowdfunding, venture debt, government grants) for different types and stages of ventures, and formulate fundraising strategies aligned with a startup’s growth needs and strategic goals. Negotiate term sheets: Demonstrate understanding of key venture deal terms (equity vs. convertible instruments, pre- vs. post-money valuation, liquidation preferences, anti-dilution provisions, control rights) and negotiate term sheets that balance investor protections with founder incentives, anticipating how terms impact future funding rounds. Manage ownership and governance: Analyze capitalization tables through multiple financing rounds, calculating founder and investor ownership dilution, and plan equity allocations (e.g. employee stock option pools). Evaluate post-investment governance issues, including board composition, investor value-add contributions, and managing founder–investor relationships. Plan exit strategies and returns: Formulate viable exit strategies for startup investments (IPO, acquisition, secondary sale, or shutdown) and assess potential outcomes. Calculate investor returns (ROI, IRR, multiples) from exit scenarios and understand how exit timing and strategy affect both founders and investors. Understand venture fund dynamics: Explain how venture capital funds operate (structure of limited partners and general partners, fund lifecycle, management fees, carried interest) and apply portfolio strategy concepts (risk diversification, follow-on reserves, “power law” returns) to make investment recommendations under the constraints of a VC fund model. Communicate investment decisions: Produce professional-quality investment documents – such as a startup valuation memo, a term sheet, or an investment pitch deck – that articulate a clear investment thesis, present due diligence findings, and justify recommendations. Defend these proposals through evidence-based reasoning and effective written and oral communication.
