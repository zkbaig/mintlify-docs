Entrepreneurial Finance – Venture Capital Perspective Course Package
## Course Overview
Entrepreneurial Finance is a graduate-level course examining how high-growth new ventures are financed from the vantage point of a venture capital (VC) investor. The curriculum explores strategies and decisions involved in funding startups, emphasizing the crucial role that venture capital plays in driving innovation and economic growth. The course covers a spectrum of financing sources for new ventures – from angel investors and venture capital firms to government programs and crowdfunding – situating each source within the broader entrepreneurial funding landscape. Venture-backed companies have an outsized impact on the economy: VC-funded startups like Amazon, Google, and Apple have grown to contribute roughly one-fifth of public market capitalization and nearly half of R&D spending in the U.S. . By focusing on North American practices with a special lens on Canada, this course highlights both the common principles of entrepreneurial finance and the unique aspects of the Canadian startup ecosystem. Students will compare how different ecosystems nurture ventures; for instance, historically only 0.5% of Canadian VC- backed startups reached valuations above $250 million versus 2.9% in the U.S. . Through this comparative approach, the course underscores the challenges and opportunities of financing entrepreneurial ventures in Canada’s growing innovation economy alongside the more mature U.S. venture market. Informed by a venture capitalist’s perspective, the course blends theory and practice to equip students with the skills to evaluate, structure, and manage startup investments. The pedagogical approach is highly interactive and experiential: each week features real-world case studies and hands-on activities such as deal simulations, financial modeling exercises, and negotiation role-plays. Students will engage with both tech and non-tech startup scenarios, ensuring a balanced understanding of diverse industry contexts. Readings from academic research and practitioner sources are combined to ground discussions in evidence-based frameworks as well as current market realities. Guest speakers—including seasoned venture capitalists, angel investors, and successful entrepreneurs—provide firsthand insights into the fundraising and investment process. By the end of the course, participants will have applied the full lifecycle of entrepreneurial finance – from opportunity screening and valuation to term sheet negotiation, portfolio management, and exit strategy – developing the acumen to think like an investor. No advanced prerequisites are required beyond basic business fundamentals; concepts are introduced from the ground up, making the course accessible to a broad audience of aspiring entrepreneurs, investors, and business professionals. 1 2 1
