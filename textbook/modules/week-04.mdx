## Learning objectives

- Explain pre-money vs. post-money valuation and how ownership percentages are derived.
- Build and interpret cap tables, including option pools and dilution.
- Evaluate how SAFEs/convertibles affect ownership at priced rounds.
- Recognize the role of negotiation, market dynamics, and exit expectations in valuation.

## Key concepts + definitions

- **VC Method:** Back-solves valuation from exit value and target return (often 10×, 30–50% annual).
- **Pre-money vs. post-money:** Value before vs. after new investment; investor % = investment/post.
- **Cap table:** Ownership ledger across founders, investors, and option pools.
- **Option pool:** Reserved equity for employees, often created pre-money.
- **Comparable multiples:** Market-based benchmarks (revenue, EBITDA, users).
- **SAFE/convertible notes:** Deferred valuation instruments with caps/discounts.
- **Unicorn:** Startup valued at $1B+.

## Main content

### Topic narrative

- Valuing startups is difficult because early ventures lack profits and stable cash flows.
- **VC Method:**
  - Estimate exit value in 5–7 years.
  - Apply target return multiple (e.g., 10×, ~58% IRR over 5 years).
  - Example: $100M exit / 10× = $10M post-money today; $50M exit / 10× = $5M post-money.
- **DCF for startups:**
  - Projections are speculative; high discount rates can make present values near zero.
  - Scenario-based DCF is more common for later-stage startups or as a sanity check.
- **Comparables:**
  - Use market multiples (e.g., SaaS at 5–10× ARR; public SaaS at 10×, private at 5–8×).
  - Example: $2M revenue × 5 = $10M valuation.
  - Per-user or EBITDA multiples can be used depending on industry.
- **Negotiation and ownership targets:**
  - Many VCs target ~20–30% ownership per round.
  - Valuation often comes down to how much capital is needed and what % investors require.
- **Cap tables and dilution:**
  - Example: founders 60/40, seed investor 20% → founders dilute to 48%/32%.
  - Example share math: Founder A 600k, Founder B 400k (1M total). $2M on $8M pre → $10M post; investor gets 250k shares (20%), total 1.25M shares.
  - Option pool (e.g., 10% post) may require ~138k new shares, diluting everyone.
- **SAFEs/convertibles:**
  - Valuation caps (e.g., $5M) defer pricing but affect future ownership.
- **Real-world context:**
  - Famous outcomes (e.g., Instagram at ~$25M early valuation, acquired for $1B) illustrate valuation jumps.
  - Canada has fewer unicorns, but activity is increasing.
- By the end, students should compute VC-method valuations, relate raise size to ownership, and read cap tables confidently.

## Worked examples

### In-class activity: Valuation and cap table role-play

- **Setup:** Teams role-play founders of DataDive Inc. and Series A investors.
- **Shared facts:** 3 founders own 100%; $1M SAFE with $5M cap; $200k revenue and growth.
- **Founder goals:** Seek ≥$8M pre, raise $2M, accept 10% option pool if required.
- **Investor goals:** Target 30% ownership for $2M, prefer ~$6–7M pre, insist on ~15% post option pool.
- **Negotiation:** Agree on pre-money and option pool; fill cap table with 1M pre shares.
- **Outcome:** Compare terms (e.g., $8M pre, $2M raise → $10M post; 20% investor, 10% pool).
- **Lesson:** Negotiation is part math, part leverage; option pools materially dilute founders.

### Case study: PharmaCo valuation negotiation

- **Background:** Montreal biotech with promising results; seed SAFE $1M at $5M cap; needs Series A.
- **Negotiation:** Founders want $20M pre; VC wants $12M; BigPharma co-invests $4M with strategic rights.
- **SAFE impact:** $1M at $5M cap converts to ~16.7% pre-Series A, diluting founders early.
- **Option pool:** VC requires 15% post pool; ~10% new pool created pre-money.
- **Scenario outcomes:**
  - $18M pre with $12M raise → $30M post; founders ~45–50%, VC ~40%, BigPharma ~13%, pool ~15%.
  - $14M pre with $12M raise → $26M post; founders can fall below 30% due to SAFE + pool dilution.
- **Exhibit summary:** Example cap table shows founders ~22–30%, SAFE ~5–6%, Series A ~52%, option pool ~15% (approximate).
- **Term snippet:** “$12M Maple Leaf, $4M BigPharma at $15M pre inclusive of 15% pool; SAFE converts at $5M cap.”

## Pitfalls / common mistakes

- Overreliance on formulas without negotiation context.
- Ignoring option pool dilution in pre-money calculations.
- Using comparables without stage or risk adjustments.
- Overestimating exit values or return multiples to justify valuations.
- Underestimating SAFE cap dilution at later rounds.

- **What is the VC method and when is it used?** Back-solves valuation from exit value and target return for early-stage companies.
- **Why is DCF less practical for startups?** High uncertainty and discount rates make outputs unreliable.
- **How do comparables work?** Apply market multiples and adjust for growth, risk, and stage.
- **How do pre- and post-money valuations relate to ownership?** Investor % = investment/post-money.
- **What is an option pool and who it dilutes?** Reserved equity, typically diluting founders pre-money.
- **How do ownership percentages shift across rounds?** Founders and early investors dilute as new money enters, ideally for a larger valuation.

## Summary + key formulas

$$
\text{Post-money Valuation} = \frac{\text{Investment Amount}}{\text{Investor Ownership}}
$$

$$
\text{Pre-money Valuation} = \text{Post-money Valuation} - \text{Investment Amount}
$$

$$
\text{VC Method (Exit Value)} = \frac{\text{Expected Exit Value}}{\text{Target Return Multiple}}
$$

- Valuation is a mix of math, market context, and negotiation.
- Cap tables translate valuation into ownership; option pools change the math.
- SAFEs/convertibles defer valuation but influence later dilution.
