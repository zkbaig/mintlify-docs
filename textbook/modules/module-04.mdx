## Key Concepts

- **Venture capital (VC) method:** Back-solves valuation from exit value and target return (often 10×, 30–50% annual), turning a future outcome into today's entry price.
- **Pre-money vs. post-money:** Value before vs. after new investment; investor % = investment/post, which sets the starting ownership split in the cap table.
- **Cap table:** Ownership ledger across founders, investors, and option pools, showing share counts and percentages after each financing event.
- **Option pool:** Reserved equity for employees, often created pre-money; it expands the share count before the new round closes and therefore dilutes existing holders.
- **Comparable multiples:** Market-based benchmarks (revenue, earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization (EBITDA), users) used to anchor valuation to peer pricing.
- **Simple Agreement for Future Equity (SAFE):** Contract that converts into equity at a future priced round, typically with a valuation cap and/or discount instead of interest or maturity; it defers pricing but still allocates future ownership.
- **Convertible notes:** Short-term debt that converts into equity at a future round, usually carrying interest and a maturity date plus a cap/discount, making conversion economics sensitive to timing and valuation.
- **Unicorn:** Startup valued at \$1B+.
- **Annual recurring revenue (ARR):** Annualized subscription revenue run rate.

## Main Content

### Chapter 1: Definitions and valuation anatomy

Valuation converts **future potential** into a **present ownership price**.

- **Pre-money:** Value before new investment; it frames the price founders are willing to sell at.
- **Post-money:** Pre-money plus new capital; it is the denominator for investor ownership.
- **Dilution:** Ownership reduction from new shares, whether from new investors, option pools, or conversions.
- **Option pool:** Reserved shares for future hires; typically negotiated as a post-money target but created pre-money, shifting dilution to existing holders.

Every valuation choice changes who owns what and who controls the board.

### When to use each method (by stage)

Before the table, remember that method selection is a stage-appropriate trade-off: the less operating history you have, the more you rely on qualitative adjustments and exit math.

| Stage | Best-fit method(s) | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Idea/Pre-seed | Scorecard/Berkus | Limited financial history; emphasize team, market size, and product risk. |
| Seed | VC method + early comps | Early traction exists, but exit math anchors expectations; comps provide a market sanity check. |
| Series A/B | VC method + comps + scenario analysis | Clearer KPIs and comparables; scenario cases help capture execution risk. |
| Growth/Late | Comps + DCF (if stable cash flows) | More predictable revenue allows cash-flow modeling; comps anchor market pricing. |
| Pre-IPO | Comps + DCF | Market comparables and public metrics dominate negotiations. |

**Interpretive summary:** Early-stage pricing is anchored by potential and risk adjustments, while later-stage pricing increasingly reflects observable market metrics and cash-flow stability.

### Chapter 3: Investor vs. founder perspective

The table below contrasts incentives, so read it as a negotiation map: each "lens" implies a different definition of a fair price and a different tolerance for dilution.

| Dimension | Founder lens | Investor lens | Implication |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Valuation | Maximize price | Optimize risk-adjusted return | Negotiation trade-offs |
| Option pool | Minimize dilution | Future hiring protection | Pool sizing debate |
| Governance | Retain control | Board oversight | Term sheet leverage |
| Timing | Raise later | Raise before cash crisis | Timing affects price |

**Interpretive summary:** Ownership outcomes are not just about price; they are shaped by control, hiring plans, and timing, which is why negotiations often trade governance or pool size for valuation.

### Chapter 4: Worked example with numbers

**Scenario:** \$2M seed on \$6M pre-money with a 10% post-money option pool.

The cap table below shows **post-money ownership** after the seed round closes. Use it to see how the option pool and new money change founder percentages, and treat the table as a snapshot that ties valuation inputs to ownership outputs.

| Holder | Ownership (post) |
| --- | --- |
| Founders | 65% |
| Option pool | 10% |
| Seed investors | 25% |

**Post-money:** \$8M. Investor ownership = \$2M ÷ \$8M = 25%.

**Key takeaway:** Even with a healthy pre-money, adding a post-money option pool and new capital compresses founder ownership quickly, so founders should model dilution before agreeing to pool size or pricing.

#### Full share count example (pre- and post-money)

**Assumptions**
- Founders start with **13,000,000 shares**.
- Target **10% post-money option pool**.
- \$2M new investment on \$6M pre-money.

**Step 1: Compute post-money valuation**
- \$6M pre + \$2M investment = **\$8M post**.

**Step 2: Solve the post-money share count**
- Post-money shares include founders + option pool + new investors.
- Let total post-money shares be **T**.

Founders = 65% of T (because 10% pool + 25% investors)  
13,000,000 = 0.65T → **T = 20,000,000 shares**

**Step 3: Derive price per share and investor shares**
- Price per share = \$8M ÷ 20,000,000 = **\$0.40**.
- Investor shares = \$2M ÷ \$0.40 = **5,000,000 shares**.
- Option pool shares = 10% of T = **2,000,000 shares**.

**Post-money ownership**
This table converts the valuation math into actual share counts so you can see the mechanics of dilution rather than just the percentages.
| Holder | Shares | Ownership |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Founders | 13,000,000 | 65% |
| Option pool | 2,000,000 | 10% |
| Seed investors | 5,000,000 | 25% |
| **Total** | **20,000,000** | **100%** |

**Interpretive summary:** The post-money share count is the bridge between price per share and ownership; once it is set, every stakeholder's percentage is mechanically determined.

#### Simple dilution timeline

1. **Day 0:** Founders 100% (13,000,000 shares).
2. **Create pool:** Founders 90%, pool 10% if created pre-money.
3. **Close seed:** Founders 65%, pool 10%, investors 25% post-money.

#### VC Method: step-by-step calculation

**Assumptions**
- Target exit in 5 years: **\$150M**
- Investor target return: **10×**
- Desired ownership at entry: **25%**
- Investment check size: **\$3M**

The steps below illustrate how exit math translates into today's valuation and why investors focus on ownership rather than just headline price.

**Step 1: Compute required value at exit**
- Investor needs \$3M × 10 = **\$30M** in exit proceeds.

**Step 2: Translate exit proceeds into ownership**
- Ownership required = \$30M ÷ \$150M = **20%**.

**Step 3: Compare to desired ownership**
- Target 25% ownership exceeds the 20% required, providing a buffer.

**Step 4: Back into post-money valuation**
- If \$3M buys 25%, post-money = \$3M ÷ 0.25 = **\$12M**.

**Step 5: Implied pre-money valuation**
- \$12M post − \$3M investment = **\$9M pre-money**.

**Interpretation**
- At \$12M post, the investor's 25% stake would be worth \$37.5M at a \$150M exit, exceeding the 10× target.  
- Founders can argue that the math supports a higher pre-money unless risk or execution concerns justify more investor ownership.

**Interpretive summary:** The VC method is ownership-driven; if the exit math clears the return target, valuation becomes a negotiation over risk rather than a purely financial constraint.

## Worked Examples

### In-class activity: Valuation and cap table role-play

- **Setup:** Teams role-play founders of DataDive Inc. and Series A investors.
- **Shared facts:** 3 founders own 100%; \$1M SAFE with \$5M cap; \$200k revenue and growth.
- **Founder goals:** Seek ≥\$8M pre, raise \$2M, accept 10% option pool if required.
- **Investor goals:** Target 30% ownership for \$2M, prefer ~\$6–7M pre, insist on ~15% post option pool.
- **Negotiation:** Agree on pre-money and option pool; fill cap table with 1M pre shares.
- **Outcome:** Compare terms (e.g., \$8M pre, \$2M raise → \$10M post; 20% investor, 10% pool).
- **Lesson:** Negotiation is part math, part leverage; option pools materially dilute founders.

Use this activity to practice translating term sheet language into ownership outcomes; the numbers are less important than the logic linking pre-money, pool size, and post-money percentages.

### Case study: PharmaCo valuation negotiation

- **Background:** Montreal biotech with promising results; seed SAFE \$1M at \$5M cap; needs Series A.
- **Negotiation:** Founders want \$20M pre; VC wants \$12M; BigPharma co-invests \$4M with strategic rights.
- **SAFE impact:** \$1M at \$5M cap converts to ~16.7% pre-Series A, diluting founders early.
- **Option pool:** VC requires 15% post pool; ~10% new pool created pre-money.
- **Scenario outcomes:**
  - \$18M pre with \$12M raise → \$30M post; founders ~45–50%, VC ~40%, BigPharma ~13%, pool ~15%.
  - \$14M pre with \$12M raise → \$26M post; founders can fall below 30% due to SAFE and pool dilution.
- **Exhibit summary:** Example cap table shows founders ~22–30%, SAFE ~5–6%, Series A ~52%, option pool ~15% (approximate).
- **Term snippet:** "\$12M Maple Leaf, \$4M BigPharma at \$15M pre inclusive of 15% pool; SAFE converts at \$5M cap."

**Interpretive summary:** The case illustrates how SAFEs and option pools compress founder ownership before new capital arrives, which is why negotiators focus on both price and conversion mechanics.
