## Key Concepts

- **Rich vs. king dilemma:** Founder trade-off between control and wealth creation.
- **Board dynamics:** How board seats translate to control and CEO retention.
- **Founder replacement:** Common outcome as firms scale (often 40%+ by initial public offering (IPO)).
- **Dual-class shares:** Founder-friendly voting structures.
- **Smart money:** Investors who add operational help beyond capital.
- **Monthly recurring revenue (MRR):** Monthly subscription revenue run rate.
- **Annual recurring revenue (ARR):** Annualized subscription revenue run rate.

### Chapter 2.1: Alignment framework (pre-capital clarity)

Use this framework to surface mismatches before you take capital. Each question should be answered on both sides, then compared for gaps.

| Alignment area | Founder questions | Investor questions | Signal to watch |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mission and pace | What growth rate preserves our culture and product quality? | What pace is required to return the fund? | Different definitions of "aggressive" growth. |
| Risk tolerance | What burn multiple are we willing to tolerate for 12–18 months? | How much downside is acceptable before we reset targets? | Comfort with volatility vs. capital efficiency. |
| Control and governance | Which decisions must stay with founders? | What decisions must require board approval? | Unclear decision rights or veto power. |
| Exit expectations | What outcomes feel like success and in what timeframe? | What exit size and timeline fits the fund model? | Misaligned return expectations or time horizons. |
| Hiring and leadership | What leadership gaps do we expect to fill and when? | How open are we to executive upgrades? | Founder replacement risk or leadership friction. |
| Support and engagement | What help do we expect beyond capital? | How hands-on do we intend to be? | Misaligned expectations on investor involvement. |

**Founder self-check (answer before taking capital):**
- What trade-offs am I willing to make to hit investor-grade growth?
- What metric would make me pause growth to protect product quality?
- What decisions am I unwilling to delegate to the board?
- What is my non-negotiable timeline for exit or independence?
- What evidence would change my mind about a pivot?

**Investor-fit questions (ask potential investors directly):**
- What is your expected return profile and timeline for this fund?
- How do you define "great" performance at our stage?
- What is your default stance on follow-on funding?
- When have you supported a founder through a pivot?
- How do you handle disagreements with founder-CEOs?

### Chapter 4: Worked example with numbers

**Scenario:** Board and key performance indicator (KPI) alignment for a growth-stage startup.

Before the board aligns on targets, everyone needs to agree **why** each metric matters: net retention signals product stickiness and expansion potential, CAC payback shows how quickly growth investments return cash, and burn multiple clarifies capital efficiency during scaling.

| Metric | Founder Target | Investor Target | Compromise |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Net retention | 110% | 120% | 115% |
| CAC payback | 10 months | 6 months | 8 months |
| Burn multiple | 2.5× | 1.5× | 2.0× |

**Takeaway:** When targets move closer together, it usually reflects shared priorities—keep customers expanding, recover acquisition costs quickly, and spend capital with discipline.

## Governance Best Practices

**Board cadence**
- **Monthly operating updates:** One-page dashboard and key decisions needed.
- **Quarterly board meetings:** 2–3 hours with pre-read sent 5–7 days prior.
- **Annual strategy reset:** Revisit goals, capital plan, and leadership roadmap.

**Metrics pack (pre-read essentials)**
- **Growth:** MRR/ARR, net revenue retention, new bookings, pipeline health.
- **Efficiency:** Burn multiple, CAC payback, gross margin trends.
- **Retention:** Logo churn, expansion vs. contraction, cohort health.
- **Cash:** Runway in months, hiring plan vs. budget.

**Investor updates**
- **Monthly email:** Summary, KPIs, wins, risks, and explicit asks.
- **Decision log:** Document board decisions and follow-up owners.
- **Escalation rule:** If runway < 9 months or churn spikes, trigger a board check-in.

## Worked Examples

### In-class activity: Board meeting role-play ("The Tense Pivot")

- **Scenario:** CloudStream faces stalled growth; investors want a pivot or exit.
- **Roles:** Founder/management, investors, independent director mediator.
- **Tension:** Founder defends consumer vision; investors push enterprise pivot.
- **Goal:** Reach a time-bound experiment or compromise plan.
- **Lesson:** Misalignment is common; independent directors can mediate.

### Case study: StartupX founder showdown

- **Background:** Series A company facing slow growth and looming cash crunch.
- **Conflict:** Lead investor proposes replacing CEO before Series B.
- **Board structure:** 2 founders, 1 venture capital (VC) investor, 1 independent; CEO change needs majority.
- **Dilemma:** Replace CEO, add a chief operating officer (COO), or delay change to preserve founder vision.

### Discussion prompts and teaching notes

- **Assess the lead investor's concerns in StartupX.**
  - Teaching note: Use performance data vs. external factors and CEO adaptability.
- **What are the consequences of removing the founder-CEO?**
  - Teaching note: Balance operational improvement vs. culture/morale risks.
- **How can this dispute be resolved constructively?**
  - Teaching note: COO compromise, clear milestones, mediation by independents.
- **How could founders/investors have prevented the showdown?**
  - Teaching note: Early expectation-setting and regular alignment checks.

### Mini-exercise: Monthly investor update outline

Build a one-page update that makes it easy for investors to diagnose health, offer help, and approve decisions.

**Checklist**
- [ ] **Headline:** One-sentence summary of momentum or risk.
- [ ] **KPIs:** Monthly recurring revenue (MRR), annual recurring revenue (ARR), net retention, burn multiple.
- [ ] **Progress:** Top 2 wins tied to milestones.
- [ ] **Risks:** Top 2 risks with mitigation plan.
- [ ] **Asks:** Specific intros, hiring help, or customer feedback.
- [ ] **Decision rights:** Any items requiring board approval.

### Conflict-resolution case: "Bridge the Boardroom"

- **Scenario:** LumaPay is 6 months from runway. The lead investor wants a hiring freeze and pricing increase; the founder wants to keep hiring in sales.
- **Roles:** CEO (founder), lead investor, independent director (mediator), CFO (data lead).
- **Goal:** Align on a 90-day plan and decision rights.

**Role-based prompts**
- **CEO:** "What outcomes do we need in 90 days to justify continued hiring?"
- **Lead investor:** "Which metrics would make you comfortable slowing burn?"
- **Independent director:** "Where do you see shared interests, and what trade-offs are acceptable?"
- **CFO:** "What are the cash impacts and downside scenarios of each option?"

**Suggested responses**
- **CEO response:** "If we hit a 4x pipeline coverage and 8-month CAC payback, we can freeze new hires after the current slate."
- **Lead investor response:** "If gross margin stabilizes at 70% and churn stays under 2%, I will support two priority hires."
- **Independent director response:** "Let's agree on two measurable milestones and revisit in 60 days with a decision memo."
- **CFO response:** "A hiring freeze adds 4 months of runway; pricing changes add 2 months but increase churn risk by 1 point."

## Pitfalls

<Callout type="warning" title="Pitfalls to Avoid">
- Avoiding transparency to "look strong," leading to trust breakdowns.
- Overlooking board composition and voting control when fundraising.
- Delaying leadership upgrades until investors force a change.
- Misaligning exit expectations and timelines early in the relationship.
</Callout>
