## Key Concepts

- **Cap table:** The ownership ledger that records every class of equity, the share counts, and each stakeholder’s percentage over time.
- **Fully diluted basis:** A view of ownership that assumes every option, warrant, and convertible instrument is exercised or converted into shares.
- **Option pool shuffle:** A negotiation tactic in which a pre-money option pool is created or resized so founders absorb additional dilution.
- **Pro forma cap table:** A forward-looking model that shows post-transaction ownership after applying new money, pools, and conversions.
- **Conversion mechanics:** The rules that describe how SAFE or convertible note terms convert into equity, including caps, discounts, and valuation impacts.

## Main Content

### Chapter 1: Definitions and dilution mechanics

Cap tables show who owns what, how many shares exist, and how each round changes control. Use them to track **ownership**, **options**, and **dilution** across time, not just at one snapshot.

- **Fully diluted shares:** The total share count after assuming all options, warrants, and convertibles are exercised or converted.
- **Pro rata rights:** The contractual right for existing investors to buy enough new shares in future rounds to maintain their ownership percentage.
- **Option pool shuffle:** The timing and size of the option pool that determine who absorbs dilution before a round closes.

<Callout type="note" title="Dilution is not always bad">
If new capital increases enterprise value faster than ownership falls, founders can still win. The math is about percentage, but the outcome is about value.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="Founder reality">
Your ownership will drop in every priced round. The goal is not to stay at 100%; the goal is to build a much larger company with a smaller slice.
</Callout>

### Chapter 3: Investor vs. founder perspective

The same dilution topics look different depending on incentives. Use this table to frame conversations before they become conflict.

| Dimension | Founder lens | Investor lens | Implication |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dilution | Preserve control | Fund company growth | Balance trade-offs |
| Pro rata | Optional | Protect winners | Follow-on economics |
| Pool size | Smaller now | Hire future team | Pool timing matters |
| SAFEs | Simple | Valuation cap risk | Convertibles shift math |

Summary: Aligning these perspectives early helps prevent conflict over ownership targets and follow-on participation. Put the trade-offs on paper before term sheets are signed.

### Chapter 4: Worked example with numbers

**Scenario:** Seed and Series A with a 15% option pool.

Use the round-by-round table to translate valuation and new money into dilution checkpoints for founders and new investors. The core equation is simple: post-money = pre-money + new money; ownership = contribution ÷ post-money.

Before reviewing the numbers, focus on how pre-money valuation and check size combine to set each round’s ownership split.

| Round | Pre-money | New Money | Post-money | Founder % (fully diluted) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Seed | \$6M | \$2M | \$8M | 65% |
| Series A | \$18M | \$6M | \$24M | 45% |

Summary: Each round compresses founder ownership; the size of the new check and the pre-money valuation jointly determine the drop. The Series A check is 25% of post-money, so founders absorb a larger step-down.

<Callout type="warning" title="Founder reality">
Each new round resets the denominator. If you do not model it round by round, the dilution surprises you later when you are negotiating control or exits.
</Callout>

**Cap table after Series A (simplified)**

The cap table below converts the round math into a clean ownership snapshot you can sanity-check with stakeholders.

| Holder | Ownership |
| --- | --- |
| Founders | 45% |
| Seed investors | 20% |
| Series A investors | 25% |
| Option pool (unallocated) | 10% |

Summary: A simplified cap table is a communication tool. It shows where ownership landed after two rounds so you can confirm everyone agrees on the same picture.

**Dilution timeline (founder ownership)**

The timeline provides a quick visual gut check to see how ownership steps down across rounds.

#### Full round-by-round cap table (shares + percentages)

**Assumptions**
- Founders start with **8,000,000 shares** so the initial cap table is simple to scale.
- **Seed:** \$2M on \$6M pre (post \$8M) with a **10% post-money option pool** created pre-money to support early hires.
- **Series A:** \$6M on \$18M pre (post \$24M) with a **15% post-money pool refresh** to sustain the next hiring phase.

**Round 0: Founding**

Use the baseline table to anchor every later dilution calculation in a concrete share count.

| Holder | Shares | Ownership % |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Founders | 8,000,000 | 100.0% |
| **Total** | **8,000,000** | **100.0%** |

Summary: The founding table sets the denominator that every subsequent round expands. Every new share issued dilutes this base.

**Round 1: Seed close (post-money)**

This table shows how the seed investors and the option pool together reduce founder ownership.

| Holder | Shares | Ownership % |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Founders | 8,000,000 | 65.0% |
| Seed investors | 3,076,923 | 25.0% |
| Option pool (unallocated) | 1,230,769 | 10.0% |
| **Total** | **12,307,692** | **100.0%** |

Summary: Founders moved from 100% to 65% because the total share count rose from 8.0M to 12.3M. The investor and the pool created 4.3M new shares, which is the dilution source.

**Before/after interpretation:** Before the seed round, founders owned all 8.0M shares. After the seed round, founders still hold 8.0M shares, but the denominator grew, so their ownership became 65%. The value of their stake can still rise if the \$8M post-money valuation grows.

<Callout type="warning" title="Founder reality">
You can keep the same share count and still lose control. Ownership is a percentage game, not a share-count game.
</Callout>

**Round 2: Series A close (post-money)**

This table adds the Series A investors and a refreshed pool to show the full dilution stack.

| Holder | Shares | Ownership % |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Founders | 8,000,000 | 43.3% |
| Seed investors | 3,076,923 | 16.7% |
| Series A investors | 4,615,385 | 25.0% |
| Option pool (unallocated) | 2,769,231 | 15.0% |
| **Total** | **18,461,538** | **100.0%** |

Summary: The share counts make dilution concrete. The Series A check and pool refresh expand the denominator from 12.3M to 18.5M shares, which pushes founder ownership down to 43.3%.

**Before/after interpretation:** Before Series A, founders held 65% and the pool was 10%. After Series A, founders still hold 8.0M shares, but the new 6.15M shares issued to Series A and the pool refresh reduced their percentage to 43.3%. The decision trade-off is simple: you accept a lower percentage to finance the next growth stage.

### Pool timing explainer (pre- vs. post-money pool)

**Setup:** Founders have **8,000,000 shares**, raise **\$2M on an \$8M pre** (post \$10M). Target a **10% option pool**.

The table below isolates the effect of pool timing so you can compare outcomes without changing any other variables.

| Pool timing | Founder % | Investor % | Pool % | What changes? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Pre-money pool** (created before financing) | 70.0% | 20.0% | 10.0% | Founders absorb pool dilution upfront. |
| **Post-money pool** (created after financing) | 72.0% | 18.0% | 10.0% | Founders + investors share the pool dilution. |

Summary: Pool timing shifts who pays for future hires, even when the pool size is identical. The pool is 10% either way; the question is whose percentage gets reduced.

**Numeric detail (pre-money pool):** Total post shares = 11,428,571. Founders 8,000,000 (70.0%), investors 2,285,714 (20.0%), pool 1,142,857 (10.0%).

**Numeric detail (post-money pool):** Investor buys 2,000,000 shares at \$1.00. After adding 1,111,111 pool shares, total = 11,111,111. Founders 72.0%, investors 18.0%, pool 10.0%.

**Before/after interpretation:** With a pre-money pool, founders drop from 100% to 70% because the pool is added before the investor buy-in. With a post-money pool, founders drop to 72% because the investor shares some of that dilution. The pool size is the same; the payer changes.

**Takeaway:** Pool timing is a negotiation lever—pre-money pools protect new investors, post-money pools spread dilution.

<Callout type="warning" title="Founder reality">
Option pools are future hires paid with your equity. The timing of the pool can cost you several percentage points of ownership.
</Callout>

### Pro rata decision mini-case

**Case: BrightLeaf Robotics**
- **Today:** Founders 60%, Seed fund 20%, Angel syndicate 10%, Option pool 10%.
- **Series A:** \$8M on \$24M pre (post \$32M). New investors want **25% post**.
- **Pro rata:** Seed fund has the right to maintain its 20% ownership.

The decision fork below shows how the same round plays out depending on whether pro rata is fully honored.

**Decision fork**
- **Allow pro rata:** Seed fund invests \$6.4M (20% of the \$32M post) and keeps 20%. Founders drop to **45%**, angels to **7.5%**, pool to **7.5%**.
- **Limit pro rata:** Seed fund invests \$3.2M (half its right). Seed drops to **10%**, founders land at **50%**, angels at **10%**, pool at **10%**, new investors increase to **30%**.

Summary: The pro rata choice reallocates dilution between insiders and new money, which changes both capital raised and relationship dynamics.

**Before/after interpretation:** If you honor pro rata, insiders take more of the round and founders end at 45%. If you limit it, new investors take more, founders end at 50%, and the seed fund ends at 10%. The trade-off is founder ownership vs. investor relationship and follow-on support.

**Founder takeaway:** Allowing pro rata can preserve supportive insiders but increases new-money size or founder dilution; limiting it preserves founder % but can strain investor relationships.

<Callout type="warning" title="Founder reality">
Pro rata is a relationship decision, not just math. A smaller check from a key insider can cost you support later.
</Callout>

### Dilution risk management checklist

- [ ] Model every round on a **fully diluted** basis so options, SAFEs, and convertibles are not overlooked.
- [ ] Tie **option pool size** to a hiring plan, not a default %, to avoid unnecessary founder dilution.
- [ ] Negotiate **pool timing** (pre vs. post) and quantify the impact so trade-offs are explicit.
- [ ] Track **ownership thresholds** (50%, 33%, 10%) for control and veto rights as financing progresses.
- [ ] Pressure-test **downside cases** (down round, flat round, bridge) to anticipate dilution shocks.
- [ ] Decide **pro rata policy** early and communicate it in the data room to reduce surprises.
- [ ] Revisit dilution quarterly as valuations and hiring change to keep forecasts current.

### In-class activity: “Dilution Dash”

- **GameCo simulation:** Founders start with 100k shares, which represents 100% ownership at formation.
- **Round 1:** An angel invests \$500k for 20%, reducing founders to 80% on a fully diluted basis.
- **Round 2:** The company creates a 10% option pool, which moves founders to 72%, the angel to 18%, and leaves 10% for the pool.

Summary: The sequence shows how new money and pools each reduce founder ownership. The founder share count can stay the same while the percentage drops.

<Callout type="warning" title="Founder reality">
