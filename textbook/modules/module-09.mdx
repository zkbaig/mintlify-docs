## Key Concepts

- **Initial public offering (IPO):** Public listing for liquidity and capital, with regulatory burdens.
- **Mergers and acquisitions (M&A) exit:** Acquisition by another company; most common VC outcome.
- **Secondary sale:** Private share sale for partial liquidity.
- **Continuation fund:** Vehicle to hold assets longer when IPO windows are closed.
- **Drag-along rights:** Governance mechanism to align exit decisions.
- **Internal rate of return (IRR):** Annualized return metric used to compare investment performance.

## Main Content

### Chapter 1: Definitions and exit routes

Exits convert **paper value** into **liquid returns**.

- **IPO:** Public listing for liquidity and capital access.
- **Acquisition:** Strategic or financial buyer purchases company.
- **Secondary sale:** Selling shares to new investors before exit.

<Callout type="note" title="Exit readiness">
Exits are not just events; they are planned outcomes aligned with cap table and governance.
</Callout>

### Chapter 2: Frameworks and decision tools

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Exit decision tree">
    - Strategic fit and buyer synergies
    - Public market readiness (scale, governance)
    - Liquidity needs of investors and founders

    ```text
    Start: choose the most realistic path
    ├─ Ready for public markets (scale + governance)?
    │  ├─ Yes → IPO (initial public offering)
    │  └─ No
    │     ├─ Strategic buyer with synergies?
    │     │  └─ Yes → M&A (mergers and acquisitions)
    │     └─ Liquidity pressure now?
    │        └─ Yes → Secondary sale
    ```
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Waterfall modeling">
    - Apply liquidation preferences
    - Distribute remaining proceeds by ownership
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

### Chapter 3: Investor vs. founder perspective

| Dimension | Founder lens | Investor lens | Implication |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Timing | Mission-driven | Fund return timing | Liquidity pressure |
| Route | Strategic alignment | Max return | Potential conflict |
| Price | Brand legacy | IRR and multiples | Negotiation dynamics |
| Control | Optional exit | Contractual rights | Drag-along matters |

### Chapter 4: Worked example with numbers

**Scenario:** \$40M acquisition, 1× participating preference, Series A owns 30%. Use the waterfall below to see how preferences change the headline outcome.

| Step | Calculation | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Preference | \$10M investment returned | \$10M to Series A |
| Remainder | \$40M - \$10M | \$30M |
| Ownership split | 30% of \$30M | \$9M to Series A |
| Total to Series A | \$10M + \$9M | \$19M |

**Takeaway:** A \$40M headline exit can translate to less than half of proceeds for common holders once preferences are applied.

## Worked Examples

### Mini-exercise: Exit memo checklist

Draft a one-page memo and confirm you captured these required inputs:

- **Market:** IPO window (public comps, volatility) and acquisition appetite (recent strategic deals).
- **Fund age:** Vintage year, remaining fund life, and distribution pressure.
- **Liquidity needs:** Founder/employee secondary interest and LP cash-return timing.

### In-class activity: Exit strategy simulation

- **Task:** VC board teams choose IPO, acquisition, or staying private longer.
- **Inputs:** Startup profile, cap table, macro environment, and LP memo.
- **Outcome:** Present rationale and risk trade-offs; compare across teams.

### Case study: SafeSpot’s dilemma

- **Company:** Cybersecurity startup with \$60M annual recurring revenue (ARR), Series C at \$500M post.
- **Options:** IPO at \$800M–\$1B; acquisition at \$750M cash; secondary at \$600M.
- **Stakeholders:** VCs own 55%, founders/employees 35%, strategic investor 10%.
- **Decision factors:** IPO fees/volatility vs. acquisition certainty; fund distributions to paid-in capital (DPI) pressure.

### Discussion prompts and teaching notes

- **Compare SafeSpot’s acquisition vs. IPO outcomes.**
  - Teaching note: Consider fees, lock-ups, and risk-adjusted value.
- **How should the board evaluate IPO timing risk?**
  - Teaching note: Weigh certainty today vs. speculative upside.
- **What strategic factors beyond price matter?**
  - Teaching note: Independence, culture, and long-term growth.
- **How do stakeholder incentives differ?**
  - Teaching note: Fund DPI pressure vs. founder prestige and team retention.
- **Is a secondary a good middle path?**
  - Teaching note: Provides liquidity but may delay decision and dilute leverage.

## Pitfalls

<Callout type="warning" title="Pitfalls to Avoid">
- Treating IPOs as the default despite M&A prevalence.
- Ignoring IPO fees, lock-ups, and market timing risks.
- Overlooking LP liquidity pressure and fund life constraints.
- Taking a secondary without alignment on long-term exit goals.
</Callout>

## Summary

- Exit strategy is a timing and alignment problem, not just valuation.
- IPOs are high-upside but high-friction; M&A is more frequent and certain.
- Secondaries and continuation funds fill liquidity gaps in slow markets.
