## Key Concepts

- **Cap table:** The ownership ledger that records every class of equity, the share counts, and each stakeholder’s percentage over time.
- **Fully diluted basis:** A view of ownership that assumes every option, warrant, and convertible instrument is exercised or converted into shares.
- **Option pool shuffle:** A negotiation tactic in which a pre-money option pool is created or resized so founders absorb additional dilution.
- **Pro forma cap table:** A forward-looking model that shows post-transaction ownership after applying new money, pools, and conversions.
- **Conversion mechanics:** The rules that describe how SAFE or convertible note terms convert into equity, including caps, discounts, and valuation impacts.

## Main Content

### Chapter 1: Definitions and dilution mechanics

Cap tables track **ownership**, **options**, and **dilution** across rounds so founders and investors can see how financing decisions change control.

- **Fully diluted shares:** The total share count after assuming all options, warrants, and convertibles are exercised or converted.
- **Pro rata rights:** The contractual right for existing investors to buy enough new shares in future rounds to maintain their ownership percentage.
- **Option pool shuffle:** The timing and size of the option pool that determine who absorbs dilution before a round closes.

<Callout type="note" title="Dilution is not always bad">
If new capital increases enterprise value faster than ownership falls, founders can still win.
</Callout>

### Pool timing explainer (pre- vs. post-money pool)

**Setup:** Founders have **8,000,000 shares**, raise **\$2M on an \$8M pre** (post \$10M). Target a **10% option pool**.

The table below isolates the effect of pool timing so you can compare outcomes without changing any other variables.

| Pool timing | Founder % | Investor % | Pool % | What changes? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Pre-money pool** (created before financing) | 70.0% | 20.0% | 10.0% | Founders absorb pool dilution upfront. |
| **Post-money pool** (created after financing) | 72.0% | 18.0% | 10.0% | Founders + investors share the pool dilution. |

Summary: Pool timing shifts who pays for future hires, even when the pool size is identical.

**Numeric detail (pre-money pool):** Total post shares = 11,428,571. Founders 8,000,000 (70.0%), investors 2,285,714 (20.0%), pool 1,142,857 (10.0%).

**Numeric detail (post-money pool):** Investor buys 2,000,000 shares at \$1.00. After adding 1,111,111 pool shares, total = 11,111,111. Founders 72.0%, investors 18.0%, pool 10.0%.

**Takeaway:** Pool timing is a negotiation lever—pre-money pools protect new investors, post-money pools spread dilution.

### Pro rata decision mini-case

**Case: BrightLeaf Robotics**
- **Today:** Founders 60%, Seed fund 20%, Angel syndicate 10%, Option pool 10%.
- **Series A:** \$8M on \$24M pre (post \$32M). New investors want **25% post**.
- **Pro rata:** Seed fund has the right to maintain its 20% ownership.

The decision fork below shows how the same round plays out depending on whether pro rata is fully honored.

**Decision fork**
- **Allow pro rata:** Seed fund invests \$6.4M (20% of the \$32M post) and keeps 20%. Founders drop to **45%**, angels to **7.5%**, pool to **7.5%**.
- **Limit pro rata:** Seed fund invests \$3.2M (half its right). Seed drops to **10%**, founders land at **50%**, angels at **10%**, pool at **10%**, new investors increase to **30%**.

Summary: The pro rata choice reallocates dilution between insiders and new money, which changes both capital raised and relationship dynamics.

**Founder takeaway:** Allowing pro rata can preserve supportive insiders but increases new-money size or founder dilution; limiting it preserves founder % but can strain investor relationships.

### Dilution risk management checklist

- [ ] Model every round on a **fully diluted** basis so options, SAFEs, and convertibles are not overlooked.
- [ ] Tie **option pool size** to a hiring plan, not a default %, to avoid unnecessary founder dilution.
- [ ] Negotiate **pool timing** (pre vs. post) and quantify the impact so trade-offs are explicit.
- [ ] Track **ownership thresholds** (50%, 33%, 10%) for control and veto rights as financing progresses.
- [ ] Pressure-test **downside cases** (down round, flat round, bridge) to anticipate dilution shocks.
- [ ] Decide **pro rata policy** early and communicate it in the data room to reduce surprises.
- [ ] Revisit dilution quarterly as valuations and hiring change to keep forecasts current.
