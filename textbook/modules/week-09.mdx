## Learning Objectives

- Compare IPOs, acquisitions, and secondary sales as exit paths.
- Evaluate how market cycles and regulation shape exit timing.
- Assess stakeholder incentives across founders, VCs, and LPs.
- Apply exit analysis to a real cap table and liquidity scenario.

## Key Concepts

- **IPO:** Public listing for liquidity and capital, with regulatory burdens.
- **M&A exit:** Acquisition by another company; most common VC outcome.
- **Secondary sale:** Private share sale for partial liquidity.
- **Continuation fund:** Vehicle to hold assets longer when IPO windows are closed.
- **Drag-along rights:** Governance mechanism to align exit decisions.

## Main Content

### Topic narrative

- VCs seek liquidity events: IPOs, M&A, and secondary sales.
- IPOs can maximize value but are costly, risky, and sensitive to market cycles.
- M&A is the most common exit; >90% of U.S. venture-backed exits are acquisitions.
- Secondary markets have grown as startups stay private longer (often 10+ years).
- Secondaries offer partial liquidity and relief when IPO windows close.
- Market conditions (e.g., 2022–2023 IPO slowdown) influence exit timing.
- Antitrust scrutiny can limit large acquisitions; easing policies can reopen M&A.
- Most startups do not achieve big exits; portfolio logic is essential.
- Alternative exits include acqui-hires, buybacks, and continuation funds.

## Worked Examples

### In-class activity: Exit strategy simulation

- **Task:** VC board teams choose IPO, acquisition, or staying private longer.
- **Inputs:** Startup profile, cap table, macro environment, and LP memo.
- **Outcome:** Present rationale and risk trade-offs; compare across teams.

### Case study: SafeSpot’s dilemma

- **Company:** Cybersecurity startup with $60M ARR, Series C at $500M post.
- **Options:** IPO at $800M–$1B; acquisition at $750M cash; secondary at $600M.
- **Stakeholders:** VCs own 55%, founders/employees 35%, strategic investor 10%.
- **Decision factors:** IPO fees/volatility vs. acquisition certainty; fund DPI pressure.

### Discussion prompts and teaching notes

- **Compare SafeSpot’s acquisition vs. IPO outcomes.**
  - Teaching note: Consider fees, lock-ups, and risk-adjusted value.
- **How should the board evaluate IPO timing risk?**
  - Teaching note: Weigh certainty today vs. speculative upside.
- **What strategic factors beyond price matter?**
  - Teaching note: Independence, culture, and long-term growth.
- **How do stakeholder incentives differ?**
  - Teaching note: Fund DPI pressure vs. founder prestige and team retention.
- **Is a secondary a good middle path?**
  - Teaching note: Provides liquidity but may delay decision and dilute leverage.

## Pitfalls

- Treating IPOs as the default despite M&A prevalence.
- Ignoring IPO fees, lock-ups, and market timing risks.
- Overlooking LP liquidity pressure and fund life constraints.
- Taking a secondary without alignment on long-term exit goals.

- **What exit route maximizes value?** Depends on growth, market, and risk tolerance.
- **Why are acquisitions most common?** IPO readiness is rare; M&A is more achievable.
- **When do secondaries make sense?** When private timelines are long and liquidity is needed.
- **How do market cycles affect exits?** Downturns close IPO windows and favor M&A/secondaries.
- **What stakeholder conflicts can arise?** Founders want control; VCs need returns; LPs need liquidity.

## Readings

### Required Readings

- **Parang (2022).** Founder reluctance to discuss exits and why planning helps.
- **Lynch (2025).** Secondary markets as mainstream VC liquidity tools.

### Optional Readings

- **Hardy (2013).** Reality check on IPO rarity and likely outcomes.
- **Reilly et al. (2022).** M&A’s importance in sustaining venture ecosystems.

## Summary

- Exit strategy is a timing and alignment problem, not just valuation.
- IPOs are high-upside but high-friction; M&A is more frequent and certain.
- Secondaries and continuation funds fill liquidity gaps in slow markets.
