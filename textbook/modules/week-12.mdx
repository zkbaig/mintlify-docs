## Key Concepts

- **Revenue-based financing (RBF):** Capital repaid as a % of revenue.
- **Venture debt:** Non-dilutive debt used to extend runway.
- **Rolling funds:** Continuous fundraising model for GPs.
- **Continuation funds:** Vehicles that extend holding periods for winners.
- **Tokenization:** Blockchain-based representations of equity or revenue shares.

## Main Content

### Chapter 1: Definitions and emerging models

The future of VC includes **new funding models**, **data-driven investing**, and **diverse capital sources**.

- **Rolling funds:** Subscription-style capital commitments.
- **Revenue-based financing:** Repayment tied to revenue.
- **SPVs:** Single-deal investment vehicles.

<Callout type="info" title="Innovation in structure">
Alternative models expand access but require new governance and transparency norms.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Speed | Fast capital | Diligence and risk | Balance pace with quality |

### Chapter 4: Worked example with numbers

**Scenario:** Revenue-based financing (RBF) vs. equity.

| Option | Terms | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| RBF | $500k for 6% of revenue until 1.5× cap | $750k repaid over 3 years |
| Equity | $500k at $5M post | 10% ownership, exit-driven return |

### Chapter 5: Summary checklist

<Steps>
  <Step title="Match model to growth">

### Discussion prompts and teaching notes

- **How should a fund balance AI and climate investments?**
  - Teaching note: Compare capital intensity, timelines, and policy tailwinds.
- **What alternative financing is best for steady-revenue startups?**
  - Teaching note: RBF or venture debt can reduce dilution.
- **Will tokenization become mainstream?**
  - Teaching note: Adoption depends on regulation and investor protections.
- **How will LP expectations shape fund models?**
  - Teaching note: LPs will reward transparency, DPI, and specialization.

## Pitfalls

- Treating new financing models as replacements rather than complements.
- Ignoring regulatory constraints for tokens and crowdfunding.
- Overconcentrating in hype sectors without diligence on unit economics.
