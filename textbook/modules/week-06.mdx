## Learning Objectives

- Distinguish economic vs. control terms in VC term sheets.
- Evaluate liquidation preference structures and their payout impact.
- Compare weighted-average vs. full-ratchet anti-dilution.
- Assess board composition and protective provisions for control.
- Recognize market cycles and how they shift term sheet norms.

## Key Concepts

- **Liquidation preference:** Priority of payout on exit (1× non-participating is standard).
- **Participating preferred:** Preference plus pro-rata participation (“double dip”).
- **Anti-dilution:** Protection against down rounds (weighted average vs. full ratchet).
- **Protective provisions:** Investor veto rights on major actions.
- **No-shop clause:** Exclusivity during diligence (binding).
- **Vesting/drag-along:** Founder retention and forced sale alignment.

## Main Content

### Topic narrative

- Term sheets are largely non-binding but set economics and control for the definitive agreements.
- **Economic terms:** valuation, option pool, liquidation preference, and anti-dilution.
- **Liquidation preference:**
  - 1× non-participating is common (investor chooses preference or conversion).
  - Participating preferred allows preference plus upside; often capped at 2–3×.
  - Multiples (2×/3×) heavily favor investors, especially in moderate exits.
- **Anti-dilution:**
  - Broad-based weighted average is standard; full ratchet is founder-unfriendly.
  - Founders can negotiate exclusions for small strategic issuances.
- **Control terms:**
  - Board composition (e.g., 2 founders, 1 investor, 2 independent) affects control balance.
  - Protective provisions require investor consent for major actions (sale, new stock, large spend).
- **Other mechanics:**
  - Founder re-vesting (often 4 years, 1-year cliff).
  - Drag-along, ROFR, pro rata rights, information rights.
- **Market cycles:**
  - Frothy markets favor founder-friendly terms; downturns increase investor-friendly terms.
  - Post-2022, more multiple prefs, participation, and stronger anti-dilution reappeared.
  - Pay-to-play clauses encourage investors to support down rounds.

## Worked Examples

### In-class activity: “The Term Sheet Tug-of-War”

- **Scenario:** DataSecure Inc. Series A term sheet with aggressive terms.
- **Negotiables:** 1× participating pref, full ratchet, investor-heavy board, 20% pre-money pool.
- **Process:** Founder team and VC team strategize, then negotiate live with instructor moderation.
- **Possible compromise:** 1× non-participating pref, weighted-average anti-dilution, 5-seat balanced board.
- **Lesson:** Term sheets are connected systems; trade-offs protect upside and control.

### Case study: NewBoard Corp term sheet

- **Deal:** $40M pre, $8M raise (16.7% ownership), but control-heavy terms.
- **Concerns:** 1× participating pref, investor-leaning board, strong protective provisions.
- **Founder re-vesting:** 50% over 3 years with a 1-year cliff.
- **Analysis:** High valuation can mask control loss; evaluate terms in moderate exits.

### Discussion prompts and teaching notes

- **How would a 1× participating pref affect payouts at $50M vs. $200M exits?**
  - Teaching note: Participation hurts most in moderate exits.
- **Does board structure give the VC control?**
  - Teaching note: Investor influence over independents can shift control.
- **Which terms should founders push back on first?**
  - Teaching note: Prefer to cap participation and avoid full ratchet.
- **Would you accept NewBoard’s deal if the VC won’t budge?**
  - Teaching note: Weigh capital need vs. long-term control and upside.

## Pitfalls

- Over-focusing on valuation while ignoring liquidation preference and control.
- Accepting full ratchet anti-dilution without understanding dilution impacts.
- Allowing investor-leaning board structures without checks.
- Treating no-shop clauses as harmless when they limit leverage.

- **What is a term sheet and is it binding?** Mostly non-binding except clauses like no-shop.
- **Why include liquidation preferences?** Downside protection for investors.
- **What is participating preferred?** Preference plus pro-rata participation (double dip).
- **How does anti-dilution work?** Weighted average is mild; full ratchet is harsh.
- **What control rights do investors gain?** Board seats, vetoes, information and pro rata rights.
- **How do market cycles affect terms?** Downturns increase investor-friendly clauses.

## Summary

- Economic terms drive payout; control terms drive governance.
- Founder-friendly defaults: 1× non-participating prefs, weighted-average anti-dilution, balanced boards.
- Negotiation leverage shifts with market cycles and company traction.
