## Key Concepts

- **Liability of foreignness:** Added friction and information asymmetry caused by distance, culture, and unfamiliar legal contexts that slow diligence and decision-making.
- **Cross-border syndicates:** Co-investments with local partners to reduce risk.
- **Regulatory constraints:** Foreign ownership, data rules, and listing restrictions.
- **Jurisdiction flips:** Delaware/Cayman holding structures for investor friendliness.
- **Foreign exchange (FX) risk:** Currency movements between investment and exit that change returns in an investor's home currency.

## Main Content

### Chapter 1: Definitions and cross-border factors

Global VC introduces **currency, legal, and market access** complexity that does not show up in domestic deals. Cross-border risk concentrates in five buckets: **information gaps, regulatory differences, governance friction, currency volatility, and exit constraints**.

- **Liability of foreignness:** Added friction and information asymmetry caused by distance, culture, and unfamiliar legal contexts.
- **Syndication:** Co-investing across regions to share risk and access local networks.
- **Foreign exchange (FX) risk:** Exchange-rate moves between investment and exit that change home-currency returns, even when the local business performs well.
- **Regulatory differences:** Data privacy, capital controls, foreign ownership rules, and tax that add legal friction and compliance cost.
- **Exit pathway variance:** Divergent M&A and IPO markets by region that constrain who can buy the company and when.

In practice, investors mitigate these risks by hiring local counsel early, building syndicates with on-the-ground leads, pricing in FX buffers or hedging where feasible, and underwriting to conservative exit paths.

<Callout type="warning" title="Cross-border friction">
Cross-border deals require more legal diligence and a longer time horizon. Expect extra contracts, slower approvals, and more time spent aligning governance across jurisdictions.
</Callout>

#### Region-by-region summary: ecosystems and funding norms

| Region | Ecosystem traits | Funding norms |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **U.S.** | Deep pools of specialized capital, fast follow-on rounds, aggressive growth targets. | USD-denominated rounds, investor-friendly terms (strong prefs, pro rata), Delaware C-corp as default. |
| **Europe** | Fragmented markets, strong technical talent, regulatory nuance by country. | Smaller average round sizes, more public co-investment, staged capital with milestone gates. |
| **Asia** | Large domestic markets, rapid scaling, policy and geopolitical sensitivity. | Strategic/corporate capital common, local investor syndicates, exits influenced by policy shifts. |
| **LATAM** | Mobile-first adoption, infrastructure gaps create leapfrog plays, macro volatility. | USD-linked pricing where possible, higher risk premiums, more structured governance to manage FX and exit risk. |

### Chapter 4: Worked example with numbers

**Scenario:** United States (U.S.) fund invests €5M in European Union (EU) startup; exit is €20M. FX moves from 1.10 to 1.00.

**Lead-in:** Use the FX return table to translate local-currency outcomes into U.S. dollar (USD) returns. The euro weakens versus the dollar (1.10 → 1.00), so each euro converts into fewer dollars at exit.

| Step | Value | U.S. dollar (USD) Return |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Investment | €5M × 1.10 | \$5.5M |
| Exit | €20M × 1.00 | \$20M |
| Gross multiple | \$20M ÷ \$5.5M | 3.64× |

**Takeaway:** Even with a strong local exit (a 4.0× euro multiple), FX shifts compress the USD multiple and change fund performance. The currency move trims roughly 0.36× off the USD outcome despite the same local cash flows. FX can erase a strong local return.

#### FX sensitivity example (EUR to USD)

Assume a €5M investment and a €20M exit. The table shows how USD returns change across FX paths.

| Scenario | Entry FX (USD/EUR) | Exit FX (USD/EUR) | USD entry | USD exit | USD multiple |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **FX tailwind** | 1.05 | 1.20 | \$5.25M | \$24.00M | 4.57× |
| **Flat FX** | 1.10 | 1.10 | \$5.50M | \$22.00M | 4.00× |
| **FX headwind** | 1.20 | 0.95 | \$6.00M | \$19.00M | 3.17× |

**Interpretation:** A strong local outcome can look mediocre in USD if FX moves against the fund. Stress-test returns and consider hedging or USD pricing when feasible.

### Cross-border structuring case: Delaware/Cayman flip

**Case:** A LATAM fintech raising a U.S.-led Series A must choose between a Delaware C-corp parent or a Cayman holding company for global expansion.

<CardGroup>
  <Card title="Delaware flip (pros)" icon="check">
    U.S. investor familiarity, clearer exit paths, easier U.S. banking and contracts.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Delaware flip (cons)" icon="triangle-exclamation">
    U.S. tax exposure, higher compliance costs, potential local incentives lost.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Cayman holdco (pros)" icon="check">
    Neutral holding structure, flexibility for multi-country ops, common in Asia.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Cayman holdco (cons)" icon="triangle-exclamation">
    Perceived opacity, U.S. investor hesitancy, additional legal complexity.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

**Decision prompts:**
- Where is the next round most likely to be led (U.S., Europe, Asia)?
- Which jurisdiction supports the most likely exit path (IPO vs. M&A)?
- How will taxes and IP ownership be handled across operating subsidiaries?
- Do local regulators restrict offshore ownership or profit repatriation?

### Mini-exercise: Cross-border risk triage

Use this checklist to pressure-test a cross-border deal before term sheet.

- [ ] **FX exposure:** Identify the base currency, hedging approach, and sensitivity to rate moves.
- [ ] **Legal structure:** Confirm jurisdiction choice, ownership rules, and enforceability.
- [ ] **Exit path:** Validate likely IPO or M&A routes and expected timelines.

### Cross-border diligence checklist

- [ ] **Entity structure:** Cap table, parent/subsidiary alignment, IP ownership.
- [ ] **Regulatory:** Licensing, data localization, foreign ownership limits.
- [ ] **Tax:** Withholding, transfer pricing, VAT/GST exposure.
- [ ] **FX & treasury:** Cash controls, hedging policy, bank access.
- [ ] **Governance:** Board composition, voting rights, minority protections.
- [ ] **Commercial:** Local contracts, customer concentration, distributor terms.
- [ ] **Exit readiness:** Listing venues, buyer appetite, FX impact on proceeds.

### Case study: SoftWear virtual reality (VR) cross-border Series B

- **Company:** Toronto-based VR startup expanding to Europe and Asia.
- **Investors:** U.S. lead, European VC, Japanese VC with valuation concerns.
- **Issues:** Delaware flip, due diligence distance, valuation gap, exit preferences.
- **Board challenges:** Time zones, strategy alignment, and governance cohesion.

### Discussion prompts and teaching notes

- **Should SoftWear VR flip to Delaware for Series B?**
  - Teaching note: Balance investor familiarity vs. local incentives and tax.
- **How can BrightEast be convinced on valuation?**
  - Teaching note: Use global comps and structured milestones.
- **What governance practices support a global board?**
  - Teaching note: Clear materials, rotating times, and independent facilitation.
- **How should the company align exit expectations?**
  - Teaching note: Discuss dual-track IPO/M&A early and revisit with market shifts.

## Pitfalls

<Callout type="warning" title="Pitfalls to Avoid">
- Assuming U.S. term sheets translate seamlessly across jurisdictions.
- Ignoring currency and exit market constraints in emerging regions.
- Overlooking cultural communication gaps in cross-border governance.
- Failing to structure legal entities for future exit flexibility.
</Callout>
