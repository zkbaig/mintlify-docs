## Key Concepts

- **Burn rate:** Net monthly cash outflow for a startup.
- **Runway:** Months of cash remaining at current burn rate.
- **Unit economics:** Revenue and cost per customer/unit, including margin and CAC/LTV.
- **CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost):** Cost to acquire a customer.
- **LTV (Lifetime Value):** Net revenue from a customer over time.
- **MRR:** Monthly recurring revenue for subscription businesses.
- **Churn:** Percentage of customers or revenue lost over a period.
- **Scenario analysis:** Base, upside, and downside forecasting to stress uncertainty.

## Main Content

### Financial projections under uncertainty

- Early-stage startups still need financial projections to plan capital needs and milestones.
- Projections are about assumptions, not precision; they show how the business model can become viable.
- Milestone-based funding helps avoid running out of cash before the next round.
- Being realistic and conservative is essential; cash shortfalls are a top cause of failure.
- Projections should connect to basic startup financial statements like the income statement and cash flow statement.
- Metrics like EBITDA or months to breakeven may be negative early on, but still provide structure for planning.

### Burn rate, runway, and cash management

- Burn rate indicates monthly cash usage; runway measures how long cash lasts.
- Founders must track burn to plan fundraising timing and milestone delivery.
- Investors evaluate how long a raise will last and what milestones it unlocks.

### Unit economics and business model viability

- Product businesses focus on gross margin per unit.
- SaaS and subscription ventures focus on CAC, LTV, ARPU, and churn.
- Investors expect a credible path from early losses to sustainable margins and scale.
- Weak unit economics (LTV &lt; CAC) signal a non-viable model unless fixed.

### Scenario analysis and critical assumptions

- Startups should model base, best, and worst cases to reveal risk.
- Identify key assumptions (conversion rates, sales cycle, pricing) and monitor them.
- Build buffers for delays and unexpected costs; funding should exceed the bare minimum.

### Metrics for investor evaluation

- MRR growth shows momentum; churn reveals retention quality.
- CAC and LTV ratios indicate acquisition efficiency.
- Burn multiple (net burn / net new ARR) compares cash use vs. growth.
- Investors use these to compare startups and spot operational weakness.

## Worked Examples

### Sample projection walkthrough

- Build a multi-year or monthly projection with revenue growth, operating expenses, and cash balance.
- Highlight slow early revenue ramps and rising staff/marketing/R&D costs.
- Use the projection to show when additional funding is required.
- Contrast startup financials (losses expected) with mature company metrics.

### In-class activity: Runway and unit economics simulation

<Callout type="info" title="Scenario snapshot: AlphaApp">
A mobile app startup has **$200k** cash, **$30k** monthly expenses, and **$10k** monthly revenue. CAC is **$10/user**, revenue is **$2/user/month**, and users grow **10% monthly** from a base of 5,000.
</Callout>

#### Goals

- Practice runway calculations and visualize how burn changes as costs and revenue scale.
- Evaluate unit economics (CAC, LTV) and decide whether to raise now or fix the model first.

#### Steps

1. **Calculate runway:** Estimate net burn and months of runway today.
2. **Project cost growth:** Add $5k/month in expenses for hiring or infrastructure and recalc runway at months 6 and 12.
3. **Check unit economics:** Compute CAC payback and LTV (use churn assumptions provided by the instructor).
4. **Make a funding call:** Decide whether to raise now or delay to hit stronger metrics.
5. **Share back:** Each group presents its runway, funding choice, and one adjustment to improve economics.

#### Debrief prompts

- What assumption (price, churn, CAC) changes runway the most?
- How do you reconcile “good” LTV/CAC with a short runway?

### Case study: FinFix Co. — financial planning for a fintech startup

#### Background

- FinFix offers a freemium personal finance app with a **$5/month** premium tier.
- Current scale: **100,000 users**, **5,000 paid** (5% conversion), growing primarily via word-of-mouth.

#### Current financials

- **Revenue:** ~$30k/month (subscriptions + referral commissions).
- **Expenses:** ~$80k/month (payroll, servers, marketing, overhead).
- **Net burn:** ~$50k/month with **~$500k cash** remaining (~10 months of runway).

#### Growth plan assumptions (next 12 months)

- Add **20k users/month** (best case), or **10k/month** (downside).
- Lift conversion from **5% → 8%** with new premium features.
- Increase referral income to **$15k/month** with deeper bank partnerships.

#### Projection highlights (Exhibit 1 summary)

- **Month 6:** ~180k users, ~$70k MRR, ~$110k expenses → ~$40k burn.
- **Month 12:** ~300k users, ~$135k MRR, ~$140k expenses → near breakeven.
- **Funding gap:** Plan implies ~**$1M** total annual burn; requires **$500k** additional capital.

#### Unit economics snapshot (Exhibit 2 summary)

- **CAC (blended):** ~$2/user today; could rise with paid marketing.
- **Premium LTV:** ~$100–$150 (2–3 year lifetime estimate).
- **Conversion:** 5% now, target 8%; LTV:CAC for premium users ~3:1.
- **Burn multiple:** Improves from ~5:1 early to ~1:1 by year-end if growth holds.

#### Investor perspective

- Investors will test whether growth assumptions are plausible and CAC stays stable as spend scales.
- Key health signals: paid conversion trend, churn, and recurring revenue momentum.

#### Decision point

- Raise in ~6 months at stronger metrics, or conserve cash and grow slower?
- Stress-test sensitivity: What if conversion only reaches **6%** or CAC doubles?

## Pitfalls

- Overestimating revenue growth without a go-to-market plan.
- Underestimating expenses, especially CAC, hiring, and overhead.
- Ignoring cash flow timing (revenue vs. cash receipts).
- Building projections with zero buffer for delays or overruns.
- Assuming profitability too early or ignoring a path to profitability entirely.

- **Why are projections valuable if they are inaccurate?** They surface assumptions, guide planning, and align fundraising to milestones.
- **What is burn rate and why does it matter?** It determines runway and survival; investors watch it closely.
- **Which unit economics matter most for startups?** Gross margin, CAC, LTV, ARPU, and churn depending on the model.
- **How should startups decide how much to raise?** Fund the next milestone plus a buffer, typically 12–18 months of runway.
- **What are common projection mistakes?** Hockey-stick revenue, missing costs, no buffer, and poor cash timing.
- **How do metrics like MRR, churn, CAC, and LTV inform investors?** They show growth efficiency and sustainability.

## Summary

**Cash burn and runway formulas**

$$
\text{Net Burn} = \text{Cash Outflows} - \text{Cash Inflows}
$$

$$
\text{Runway (months)} = \frac{\text{Cash on Hand}}{\text{Net Burn per Month}}
$$

- Projections translate assumptions into a roadmap and fundraising plan.
- Scenario analysis exposes downside risk and informs buffer sizing.
- Investors focus on burn, runway, and unit economics to judge viability.
- Metrics like MRR, churn, CAC, and LTV describe growth quality and efficiency.
