## Learning Objectives

- Compare VC method, DCF, and comparables for early-stage valuation.
- Explain how qualitative factors (team, market, traction) shape valuation.
- Compute pre-money and post-money ownership outcomes.
- Assess how option pools and market conditions affect valuations.
- Recognize valuation risks such as down rounds and anti-dilution terms.

## Key Concepts

- **Terminal value:** Forecast exit value used in VC method.
- **Discount rate:** Risk-adjusted rate used to discount future value (often 30–60% for VC).
- **Berkus Method:** Heuristic valuation assigning value to idea, prototype, team, etc.
- **Down round:** New round priced below the prior valuation.
- **Anti-dilution:** Terms that protect earlier investors in down rounds.
- **Pre-money vs. post-money:** Determines ownership percentages after investment.

## Main Content

### Chapter 1: Definitions and valuation lenses

Valuation is a **range** informed by method choice and assumptions.

- **DCF:** Discount future cash flows by a high risk rate.
- **Comparables:** Apply revenue/user multiples from similar deals.
- **VC method:** Back-solve from exit value and target return.

<Callout type="info" title="Triangulate, don’t anchor">
No single method is “right”; use multiple methods to bracket a range.
</Callout>

### Chapter 2: Frameworks and decision tools

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="DCF logic">
    - Forecast cash flows (years 1–5)
    - Apply terminal multiple
    - Discount at 30–60% for startup risk
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Comparables logic">
    - Pick comparable companies
    - Choose relevant multiple (ARR, users)
    - Adjust for growth and margin differences
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

### Chapter 3: Investor vs. founder perspective

| Dimension | Founder lens | Investor lens | Implication |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Forecasts | Optimistic growth | Base case with risk | Sensitivity checks |
| Multiples | Best peer comps | Median or discounted comps | Range negotiation |
| Risk | Vision upside | Downside protection | Terms matter |
| Timing | Raise at peak | Price with cycle risk | Market windows |

### Chapter 4: Worked example with numbers

**Scenario:** $1M ARR SaaS with 100% YoY growth.

| Method | Assumption | Value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Comps | 6× ARR | $6M |
| VC method | $60M exit ÷ 10× | $6M post-money |
| DCF | $1.5M FCF by Year 5, 40% discount | ~$5M |

**Range:** $5M–$7M post-money before term adjustments.

## Worked Examples

### In-class activity: “Valuing a Venture – Art or Science?”

- **Scenario:** TechNova Inc. (SaaS) with $2M current revenue, 100% YoY growth, $20M projected revenue in 5 years.
- **Data:** Industry P/E ~12×, recent acquisitions ~5× revenue.
- **Calculations:**
  - Comparables: 5× current revenue vs. 5× projected revenue.
  - DCF: use profit margins and 40% discount rate with 12× terminal multiple.
  - VC method: 5× $20M exit = $100M; 10× target return → $10M post-money.
- **Discussion:** Adjust valuations for team strength and competition; compare ranges ($10M vs. $50M+).
- **Outcome:** Students see valuation variance and the role of assumptions.

### Case study: HealthRx valuation dilemma

- **Background:** Digital health startup with $200k ARR and early pilots; not yet profitable.
- **Term Sheet A:** $10M post-money for $2M investment (20% equity); justified by comparables.
- **Term Sheet B:** $15M post-money for $2M (13.3% equity) but with milestone-triggered anti-dilution.
- **Founder dilemma:** Lower valuation with clean terms vs. higher valuation with punitive conditions.
- **Context:** Founders expected ~$20M based on hype; must evaluate realistic valuation and partner fit.

### Discussion prompts and teaching notes

- **Which HealthRx term sheet is more justified and why?**
  - Teaching note: Use ARR multiples, risk, and partner value to justify A vs. B.
- **How do milestone-based anti-dilution terms change real valuation?**
  - Teaching note: They can reprice the deal lower if targets are missed.
- **What qualitative factors justify a higher valuation?**
  - Teaching note: Patents, strong team, strategic interest, large market.
- **What risks come with overvaluation?**
  - Teaching note: Down rounds, underwater options, and reputational damage.
- **What deal would you recommend?**
  - Teaching note: Consider clean terms vs. inflated valuation and investor fit.

## Pitfalls

- Treating valuation as a single “correct” number rather than a range.
- Ignoring option pool dilution when negotiating pre-money.
- Accepting high valuations with punitive milestones or anti-dilution terms.
- Using hockey-stick projections without credibility.
- Underestimating the impact of market cycles on pricing.

- **What methods are commonly used to value startups?** DCF, comparables, and the VC method.
- **Why do VCs demand 10×+ returns?** Portfolio failure rates require a few outsized wins.
- **How do pre- and post-money valuations relate?** Post-money = pre + investment; ownership = investment/post.
- **What qualitative factors influence valuation?** Team, market size, technology, and traction.
- **How do market conditions shift valuation?** Booms inflate; downturns tighten and lead to flat/down rounds.
- **What is a down round and why avoid it?** Lower valuation later round; harms morale and triggers anti-dilution.

## Readings

### Required Readings

- **Sahlman & Scherlis (2009). VC Method note.** Forecast exit, apply high discount rate, and choose terminal multiples.
- **Darden note (2016). Valuing the Early-Stage Company.** Comparables, NPV, decision trees, and qualitative judgment.
- **Payne (2007). VC Method for pre-revenue.** Step-by-step terminal value and ROI calculations.

### Optional Readings

- **Brex Journal (2021).** Eight valuation methods; Berkus Method details (up to $500k per factor, ~$2M cap).
- **Equidam (2023).** Methods to use/avoid; warns against earnings/book-value methods for startups.

## Summary

- Valuation methods triangulate across DCF, comparables, and VC method assumptions.
- High discount rates and return multiples reflect VC risk and dilution.
- Term-sheet terms can make a “high valuation” economically worse.

    **Answer:** High failure risk and long time horizons.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Comparables">
    **Question:** If ARR is $2M and the peer multiple is 8×, what is implied value?

  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="VC method">
    **Question:** If exit is $120M and target return is 12×, what post-money value fits a $5M check?  
    **Answer:** $10M post-money (investor needs $60M; $60M ÷ $120M = 50% ownership → $5M ÷ 50% = $10M).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Range setting">
    **Question:** Why should founders avoid anchoring on a single method?  
    **Answer:** Different methods capture different risks and can vary widely.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Down round risk">
    **Question:** What is one consequence of overvaluation?  
    **Answer:** Increased risk of a down round and morale damage.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Term trade-offs">
    **Question:** Which term might you give up for a higher valuation?  
    **Answer:** Stronger liquidation preference or board control (hint: avoid if too punitive).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
