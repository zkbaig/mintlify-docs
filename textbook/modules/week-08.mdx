## Key concepts + definitions

- **Rich vs. king dilemma:** Founder trade-off between control and wealth creation.
- **Board dynamics:** How board seats translate to control and CEO retention.
- **Founder replacement:** Common outcome as firms scale (often 40%+ by IPO).
- **Dual-class shares:** Founder-friendly voting structures.
- **Smart money:** Investors who add operational help beyond capital.

## Main content

### Topic narrative

- Investor–founder dynamics balance control, expectations, and incentives.
- As rounds accumulate, investor equity and board seats shift control away from founders.
- Core tension: founders want vision and culture; VCs push for scale and exit timelines.
- CEO replacement is common; studies show ~40% of VC-backed IPOs have different CEOs.
- Examples: Steve Jobs (Apple) and Travis Kalanick (Uber) were pushed out by boards.
- Common tension points:
  - Strategy pivots and market direction.
  - Hiring “professional” executives over founder leadership.
  - Exit timing misalignment (VC fund life vs. founder patience).
- Communication is essential; transparency avoids surprises and builds trust.
- Healthy dynamics treat VCs as partners and coaches, not bosses.
- Diluted founders (&lt;5%) may need equity refreshes to maintain motivation.
- Cultural norms differ by region, with Silicon Valley more accepting of founder replacement.

## Worked examples

### In-class activity: Board meeting role-play (“The Tense Pivot”)

- **Scenario:** CloudStream faces stalled growth; investors want a pivot or exit.
- **Roles:** Founder/management, investors, independent director mediator.
- **Tension:** Founder defends consumer vision; investors push enterprise pivot.
- **Goal:** Reach a time-bound experiment or compromise plan.
- **Lesson:** Misalignment is common; independent directors can mediate.

### Case study: StartupX founder showdown

- **Background:** Series A company facing slow growth and looming cash crunch.
- **Conflict:** Lead investor proposes replacing CEO before Series B.
- **Board structure:** 2 founders, 1 VC, 1 independent; CEO change needs majority.
- **Dilemma:** Replace CEO, add COO, or delay change to preserve founder vision.

## Pitfalls / common mistakes

- Avoiding transparency to “look strong,” leading to trust breakdowns.
- Overlooking board composition and voting control when fundraising.
- Delaying leadership upgrades until investors force a change.
- Misaligning exit expectations and timelines early in the relationship.

- **How does power shift as startups raise capital?** Investors gain equity and board control over time.
- **Why do investors replace founder-CEOs?** Scaling challenges and execution risk.
- **What drives founder–VC conflict?** Strategy, hiring, burn rate, and exit timing.
- **How can founders maintain control?** Governance design, aligned investors, and transparency.
- **What positive dynamics can occur?** Smart money partnerships and mentorship.
- **What happens when conflict escalates?** Boards can replace CEOs; founder departures harm morale.

## Summary + key formulas

- Founder–investor relationships hinge on alignment, communication, and governance.
- CEO replacement is common; founders should plan for leadership evolution.
- Board control and investor timelines shape key decisions.
